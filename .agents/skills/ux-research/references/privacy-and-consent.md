# Privacy and consent

Apply throughout the study, from screener to archived shareout.

## Consent

Collect informed consent before any data collection, and reconfirm verbally before recording starts.

Consent must state:

- Who is running the study and why
- What will happen during the session and how long it takes
- Whether the session is recorded, and what is recorded (audio, video, screen)
- Who can access the raw data and for how long it is kept
- How findings will be reported and that they will be anonymized
- That participation is voluntary, that any question may be skipped, and that the participant may stop at any time without consequence
- How to withdraw afterwards and who to contact

For internal employee research, add explicitly that participation and responses are not a performance evaluation, are not shared with the participant's manager in identifiable form, and have no effect on their role.

Never start recording before an explicit yes. If a participant declines recording, continue with notes only.

## Data minimization

Collect only what the research questions require.

Never request:

- Passwords, tokens, or authentication credentials
- Precise home addresses
- Payment card, bank, or financial credentials
- Health, religious, political, union, or sexual-orientation data unless the study is formally scoped and approved for it
- Anything unrelated to the stated research objective

If a participant volunteers sensitive information spontaneously, do not probe it further and exclude it from notes and deliverables.

## Screen sharing and live data

- Ask participants to close unrelated tabs, messaging apps, and notifications before sharing a screen.
- Prefer test accounts and staging data over real production records.
- If real customer or colleague data becomes visible, pause, ask the participant to hide it, and do not capture it in notes or recordings.

## Anonymization

- Assign a participant ID (P1, P2, …) at recruitment and use it everywhere.
- Keep the ID-to-identity mapping in a separate access-restricted location, never in transcripts, decks, or tickets.
- Redact names, emails, phone numbers, employee IDs, customer IDs, account numbers, team names that identify one person, and any unique detail that makes the participant recognizable.
- Replace redactions with typed placeholders, for example `[NAME]`, `[EMAIL]`, `[TEAM]`, `[CUSTOMER_ID]`.
- Check quotes individually: a verbatim can identify someone even when the name is removed.

## Storage and retention

- Store recordings and raw transcripts in access-controlled storage limited to the research team.
- Set and state a retention period, delete raw recordings once analysis is complete, and keep only anonymized artifacts afterwards.
- Do not paste raw, unredacted transcripts into shared documents, tickets, chat channels, or third-party tools.
- Do not send participant data to external services that have not been approved for it.

## Participant wellbeing

- Reinforce that the tool is under test, not the participant.
- Allow skipping any question and stopping at any moment.
- Watch for distress or fatigue and end the session early if needed.
- Avoid coercion: no pressure from managers, no implied consequence for declining.
- For internal studies, exclude participants who directly built the artifact under test to avoid conflict of interest and social pressure.

## Escalate before collecting data when

- Consent language, recording permission, or retention rules are unclear or missing.
- The study touches minors, health data, or other special-category data.
- Data would leave the approved environment or cross a region with different rules.
- A participant's safety, employment, or standing could be affected by their answers.
