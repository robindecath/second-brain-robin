# Session moderation and protocol design

Covers building the session material, auditing it for bias, and running the session.

Build the protocol with [../assets/session-protocol-template.md](../assets/session-protocol-template.md).

## Required context before drafting

1. Research objectives and hypotheses
2. Target sample: role, segment or business unit
3. Existing draft or materials, if any
4. Session format: interview, usability test, or mixed
5. Session duration and output language

Check the conversation first, ask only for what is missing, then stop and wait. Iterate on the draft until the user confirms it is right before producing the final formatted document.

## Methodological rules

1. **Attitudinal vs behavioral firewall.** A prototype or flow is evaluated by watching someone attempt a real task, never by asking whether they like it or would use it. If the objective is usability, the protocol must contain tasks with observable success criteria.
2. **Performance-evaluation bias.** Open with an explicit reassurance: the tool is being tested, not the participant, and anything confusing is a fault of the design.
3. **Hierarchy and social-desirability bias.** Normalize criticism instead of inviting validation. Replace "Is this clear?" with "Walk me through what you expect to happen here" and "What would make this annoying on a busy day?".
4. **Past behavior before opinion.** Ask what the person actually did the last time before asking what they think or would do.
5. **One question at a time.** No double-barreled or stacked questions.
6. **No solution pitching.** Do not describe the intended benefit before the participant has reacted.
7. **Order effects.** Randomize or counterbalance task order when comparing options, and note fixed order as a limitation when it cannot be varied.
8. **Time budget.** Assign an estimated duration to every section and keep the total under the booked length, leaving room for consent and wrap-up.

## Bias audit

Run this audit on every protocol before use, and report it with the draft.

| Check | Fail looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leading | "How much easier is this version?" | "How does this version compare to what you do today?" |
| Loaded | "Do you struggle with the old form?" | "Tell me about the last time you filled in this form." |
| Double-barreled | "Was it fast and clear?" | Split into two questions. |
| Hypothetical | "Would you use this weekly?" | "How often did you do this in the last month?" |
| Validation-seeking | "That makes sense, right?" | "What, if anything, doesn't match your expectation?" |
| Attitudinal usability | "Do you think you could find it?" | "Find X. I'll watch." |
| Jargon | Internal product or team vocabulary | Use the participant's own words. |
| Missing observation criteria | Task with no defined success | State exactly what to observe or measure. |

## Session structure

1. **Welcome and consent (2–3 min).** Purpose, duration, recording permission, confidentiality, no right answers, tool under test not the person, freedom to skip or stop.
2. **Warm-up (3–5 min).** Role, context, and recent relevant behavior. Builds rapport and calibrates vocabulary.
3. **Core (bulk of the session).** Behavioral questions or tasks tied one-to-one to research questions.
4. **Wrap-up (5 min).** Anything missed, biggest friction, single change they would make, thanks, next steps.

For each core task or section, define:

- **Objective:** which research question it serves
- **Verbal instruction:** the exact short phrasing to read aloud
- **Elements to observe or measure:** specific behaviors, errors, hesitations, workarounds, success criteria
- **Probing prompts:** follow-ups to use if the participant goes quiet or stays surface-level
- **Estimated time**

## Probing toolkit

- **Permission to critique.** "The team can't hear you, and I didn't design this — what's annoying about it?"
- **Boomerang.** Return their own word as a question: "You said it was 'a hassle' — a hassle how?"
- **Silent pause.** Wait three to five seconds after an answer. Most of the real answer comes next.
- **Last-time probe.** "When did that last happen? Walk me through it."
- **Consequence probe.** "What happened after that? What did it cost you?"
- **Workaround probe.** "What do you do instead when it fails?"
- **Expectation probe.** "Before you clicked, what did you expect to happen?"

## Note taking

- Record observation and interpretation in separate columns or fields.
- Timestamp notable moments so the recording can be revisited.
- Capture verbatims word for word; never paraphrase inside quotation marks.
- Log task outcome as success, success with difficulty, or failure, with the cause.
- Note anything that surprised you — it is the seed of the next insight.

## Moderator discipline

- Do not explain the interface unless the participant is fully blocked, and note it when you do.
- Do not defend the design or correct the participant.
- Do not finish their sentences or supply the word they are searching for.
- Stay neutral in tone: avoid "great", "exactly", "perfect" as reactions to content.
- Keep the objective fixed; adapt only the follow-up questions.
- If a participant is distressed, fatigued, or blocked, move on or end early.

## Pilot

Run one pilot session before the real fieldwork. Check timing, comprehension of instructions, prototype stability, recording setup, and note template. Fix the protocol before session two.
