# Recruitment and screening

Covers screener design and participant outreach, for external users and for internal colleagues (B2E).

## Required context before writing anything

1. Research objective and the decision it informs
2. Target profile: role, business unit or segment, seniority, relevant behavior and exposure
3. Number of participants and how many distinct groups
4. Session duration, format, and window of availability
5. Output language

Check the conversation first. Ask only for what is missing, then stop and wait.

## Screener design

Build the questionnaire with [../assets/screener-template.md](../assets/screener-template.md).

Rules:

1. **Screen on behavior, not titles.** Ask what the person did recently and how often, not whether they consider themselves a user.
2. **Hide the desired answer.** Offer balanced option lists with plausible distractors so people cannot guess what qualifies them.
3. **Filter conflicts of interest.** Reject anyone who designed, built, or owns the artifact under test, and anyone on the research team.
4. **Filter professional respondents** for external recruitment: exclude people who took part in a study for the same product in the last few months.
5. **Ask one thing per question.** No double-barreled items.
6. **Keep it short.** Aim for 6–10 questions and under three minutes.
7. **Tag every answer option** with `[QUALIFIED]`, `[REJECT]`, or `[NEUTRAL]` in the internal version, and remove those tags from the version participants see.
8. **Capture quota variables** (segment, tenure, frequency, device, region) so you can balance the sample.
9. **Be transparent** about purpose, duration, recording, and confidentiality in the intro, and link the consent terms.
10. **Collect the minimum contact data** needed to schedule, and nothing more.

For internal studies, avoid questions that invite political friction, such as rating a colleague's team or tool ownership. Keep the framing on the participant's own workflow.

## Outreach

Build messages with [../assets/outreach-kit-template.md](../assets/outreach-kit-template.md).

Rules:

- Lead with what the participant gains: influence over a tool or experience they use.
- State duration, format, and what they will be asked to do, in the first three lines.
- Say clearly that there is no preparation and no right answers, and that the tool is being tested, not them.
- State recording and confidentiality upfront.
- Make the response action a single click or link.
- Keep the message short enough to read on a phone.
- Never imply obligation, urgency pressure, or evaluation.

Incentives:

- External participants: offer a fair, clearly stated incentive, and state it in the invitation.
- Internal colleagues: do not offer monetary incentives. Emphasize impact on their daily work and commit to sharing back results.

## Scheduling and no-show prevention

- Use a self-service booking link with real availability rather than back-and-forth email.
- Calendar invite must carry: clear title, duration, meeting link or room, short agenda, the psychological-safety reminder, and consent or recording information.
- Enable automatic reminders 24 hours before.
- Check RSVPs 24 hours before and send one friendly nudge to anyone pending.
- For internal studies, have the product manager align with the participant's manager so the time is protected.
- Over-recruit by roughly 20 percent, or keep a short backup list, to absorb no-shows.
- Send a short thank-you after the session, restating confidentiality and when results will be shared.

## Sample quality check

Before starting fieldwork, confirm:

- The recruited sample matches the users affected by the decision.
- Each distinct group has enough participants to see a pattern.
- No participant has a conflict of interest.
- Quotas cover the segments the team will want broken out.
- Known sampling bias is written down so it can be reported with the findings.
